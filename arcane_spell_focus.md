# Arcane Spell Focus

## Overview

Arcane Spell Focus items are specialized magical implements that allow mages to store spell patterns and cast them by supplying mana. Unlike scrolls which are consumed on use, these artifacts can be used repeatedly as long as the mage has sufficient mana to power them.

The mage focuses spells into the artifact during preparation, effectively "loading" the spell patterns. During combat or exploration, the mage can cast these stored spells instantly by channeling mana through the focus, bypassing the normal spell preparation time.

---

## Spell Focus Types

### Spellbook
**Storage Capacity:** Large (20-50+ spells depending on book quality)
**Durability:** Fragile - Vulnerable to fire and water damage

**Item Variations:**
- **Pocket Book:** 0.5x normal capacity (10-25 spells) - Highly portable, can be used in combat easily
- **Standard Spellbook:** 1x capacity (20-50 spells) - Balanced between portability and storage
- **Heavy Tome:** 2x-3x capacity (40-150 spells) - Lab use only, too heavy and cumbersome for combat or travel, requires desk/lectern

**Special Properties:**
- Can store the widest variety of spells
- Allows spell organization and reference
- Some books can be enchanted with protective magic (fire-proof, water-proof)
- Higher quality books allow more complex spell notation
- Can be passed down or traded to other mages
- Tomes are typically used in magical laboratories for preparing complex rituals and high-level spells

**Weaknesses:**
- Highly vulnerable to fire damage
- Takes up inventory space
- Can be stolen or lost
- Requires both hands to read during preparation
- Heavy Tomes cannot be used in combat situations

**Crafting Requirements:**
- Parchment/Paper
- Leather binding or wood covers
- Magical ink
- Optional: Protective enchantments

---

### Crystal Ball (Scrying Orb)
**Storage Capacity:** Limited (5-10 spells)
**Durability:** Fragile - Can shatter and explode if damaged

**Item Variations:**
- **Crystal Ball/Orb:** Standard multi-spell storage (5-10 spells), general use across spell schools
- **Specialized Crystals/Gems:** Single-spell focus with AMPLIFIED power
  - Ruby/Fire Crystal: Fire spells only, significant damage boost
  - Sapphire/Ice Crystal: Ice/Water spells only, enhanced freezing effects
  - Emerald/Nature Crystal: Nature/Healing spells only, improved restoration
  - Amethyst/Mind Crystal: Psychic/Illusion spells only, stronger mental effects
  - Topaz/Lightning Crystal: Lightning/Storm spells only, increased electrical damage
  - Diamond/Pure Crystal: Any single spell, universal amplification

**Special Properties:**
- Focuses certain schools of magic more effectively (Divination, Illusion, Mind magic)
- Provides bonus to spell power for attuned magic types
- Can be used for scrying and divination
- Crystalline structure amplifies magical resonance
- **Specialized crystals trade versatility for raw power** - storing only one spell type but with significantly enhanced effects

**Weaknesses:**
- Very fragile - will shatter if dropped or struck
- Explodes when broken, potentially harming the wielder
- Limited spell storage
- Expensive to craft or replace
- Specialized crystals cannot store spells outside their element

**Crafting Requirements:**
- Large clear crystal or glass sphere
- Magical essence infusion
- Precision enchanting
- Optional: Elemental attunement crystals or specific gemstones

---

### Staff
**Storage Capacity:** Limited (8-15 spells)
**Durability:** Moderate - Wood can crack but is repairable
**Special Properties:**
- Can be used as a melee weapon in combat
- Allows mixing of combat and spellcasting
- Can channel more raw magical power
- Some staves grant passive bonuses
- Can be used as a walking stick/tool

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited spell storage compared to books
- Two-handed weapon (limits shield use)
- Wooden staves vulnerable to fire
- Can be sundered by heavy weapons

**Crafting Requirements:**
- Quality wood (oak, ash, yew, or magical wood)
- Crystal or gem focus point
- Metal reinforcement (optional)
- Runic inscriptions

---

### Amulet
**Storage Capacity:** Very Limited (1 medium spell OR 2-3 small spells)
**Durability:** Good - Small and protected under clothing

**Special Properties:**
- Hands-free operation
- Hidden from view (harder to identify as mage)
- Passive magical protection when worn
- Can be combined with other spell focuses
- Quick-access to emergency spells
- **Material and gemstone quality determines maximum spell level:**
  - Silver + Basic Gem: Small spells only
  - Gold + Quality Gem: Small or Medium spells
  - Platinum + Perfect Gem: Medium or Large spells

**Weaknesses:**
- Very limited spell storage
- Can be grabbed/torn off in combat
- Small size limits magical power output
- Requires neck slot (competes with other jewelry)
- Poor quality materials cannot hold powerful spells

**Crafting Requirements:**
- Precious metal (silver, gold, platinum)
- Gemstone or crystal
- Fine chain or cord
- Enchantment ritual

---

### Ring
**Storage Capacity:** Minimal (1 medium spell OR 2-3 small spells per ring)
**Durability:** Excellent - Small and very durable

**Special Properties:**
- Can wield two rings at once (one per hand)
- Extremely portable and concealable
- Difficult to disarm or remove in combat
- Can be used with any other spell focus
- Allows finger-pointing gestures for precise targeting
- **Material and gemstone quality determines maximum spell level:**
  - Silver + Basic Gem: Small spells only
  - Gold + Quality Gem: Small or Medium spells
  - Platinum + Perfect Gem: Medium or Large spells

**Weaknesses:**
- Minimal spell storage per ring
- Requires finger slots (limits other magical rings)
- Small magical capacity
- Expensive for limited benefit
- Poor quality materials cannot hold powerful spells

**Crafting Requirements:**
- Precious metal band
- Small gemstone or rune inscription
- Miniature enchantment work
- High precision jeweler crafting

---

### Rune Tattoo
**Storage Capacity:** Very Limited (3 small spells OR 1 medium spell per tattoo)
**Durability:** Permanent - Cannot be removed or destroyed

**Item Variations:**
- **Small Rune Tattoo:** Can hold 3 small spells, takes less body space, lower HP cost
- **Medium Rune Tattoo:** Can hold 1 medium spell, takes more body space, moderate HP cost
- **Large Rune Tattoo:** Can hold 1 large/powerful spell, takes significant body space, high HP cost
- Tattoo size and complexity determines capacity and HP sacrifice required

**Special Properties:**
- **PERMANENT** - Once inscribed, cannot be unlearned or changed
- Spells are of significantly higher quality and power
- No equipment slot required - literally part of the mage's body
- Cannot be disarmed, stolen, or lost
- Can be used simultaneously with ALL other spell focus types
- Instant casting - no need to hold or manipulate an object
- Spells grow stronger as the mage's understanding deepens

**Weaknesses:**
- **HP COST** - Mage takes permanent damage when rune is inscribed
- Extremely limited capacity (body can only hold so many tattoos)
- Cannot be removed - poor spell choice is permanent
- Painful inscription process
- Visible marks may reveal mage's capabilities to enemies
- Scarring can interfere with additional tattoos

**Inscription Requirements:**
- Master runecrafter or tattooist
- Magical inks (often mixed with creature blood or essences)
- Ritual preparation
- **HP sacrifice** from the mage (permanent health reduction)
- Days or weeks of recovery time
- High-quality spell to inscribe (tattoo quality matches spell quality)

**Special Notes:**
- Most mages limit themselves to 3-5 rune tattoos maximum
- Common strategy: Inscribe your most-used or signature spells
- Elite mages may have powerful combat spells tattooed for emergencies
- Some cultures view rune tattoos as marks of dedication/mastery
- Can create "spell combos" if tattoos are placed strategically on the body

## Design Philosophy

Each spell focus offers different trade-offs:

**Capacity vs Safety:**
- Spellbooks offer maximum storage but are vulnerable
- Crystal Balls offer power but can explode
- Staves balance utility with combat capability

**Flexibility vs Power:**
- Books allow the most versatility
- Rune Tattoos offer the highest quality but zero flexibility
- Rings/Amulets provide emergency backup options

**Risk vs Reward:**
- Rune Tattoos: Permanent HP loss for permanent power gain
- Crystal Balls: Explosion risk for enhanced magical focus
- Spellbooks: Fire vulnerability for vast spell libraries

**Complementary Systems:**
- Mages can combine multiple focus types
- Example: Spellbook (main arsenal) + Ring (emergency spell) + Rune Tattoo (signature spell)
- Example: Staff (combat mage) + Amulet (defensive spell) + Rune Tattoo (ultimate attack)

## Mana System

All spell focuses operate on the same principle:
1. **Preparation Phase:** Mage focuses spell patterns into the artifact
2. **Storage:** Artifact holds the spell pattern indefinitely
3. **Casting:** Mage supplies mana to activate the stored spell
4. **Reuse:** Spell pattern remains stored, can be cast again with more mana

This system allows mages to:
- Prepare spells during downtime
- Cast quickly in combat without preparation
- Use spells beyond their current skill level (if stored earlier)
- Share spellbooks or staves with other mages (if compatible)

## Upgrade Paths

### Spellbook Upgrades
- Fireproof enchantment
- Waterproof binding
- Expanded pages (more spell capacity)
- Quick-reference tabs
- Self-organizing spell index
- Locked binding (prevents theft)

### Crystal Ball Upgrades
- Reinforced structure (harder to shatter)
- Elemental attunement (focus specific magic schools)
- Scrying range enhancement
- Multi-faceted (store spells in different facets)
- Levitation enchantment (follows mage)

### Staff Upgrades
- Metal reinforcement (combat durability)
- Blade attachment (spear-staff hybrid)
- Additional crystal focuses
- Elemental channeling
- Extended length (reach weapon)

### Amulet Upgrades
- Additional gemstone sockets
- Chain of holding (harder to remove)
- Passive shielding enhancement
- Soul-binding (returns if lost)

### Ring Upgrades
- Band material (gold → platinum → mithril)
- Gemstone quality (larger stones = more power)
- Multi-stone setting (multiple spells)
- Resize enchantment (fits any finger)

### Rune Tattoo Upgrades
- **Cannot be upgraded directly**
- Mage can inscribe additional tattoos (more HP cost)
- Existing tattoos grow stronger with mage's level
- Can be enhanced with magical oils/rituals temporarily
- Overlapping tattoo patterns (advanced technique, extreme risk)

## Non-Focus Items as Spell Storage

While dedicated spell focus items are optimized for storing and channeling spells, **regular items can technically store spells but are very inefficient:**

- **Storage Capacity:** Usually limited to only 1 spell
- **Inefficiency:** Requires significantly more mana to cast (25-50% increased cost)
- **Instability:** Spells may degrade over time or fail to cast properly
- **Examples:**
  - A warrior's sword with a flame spell embedded
  - A cloak with a single invisibility spell
  - A hammer with a thunder strike enchantment
  - Boots with a single speed enhancement

**Why Use Non-Focus Items for Spells?**
- Concealment: Enemies won't expect a spell from a mundane item
- Backup: Emergency spell when focus items are unavailable
- Specialization: Weapon-specific enchantments for hybrid combat styles
- Surprise: Tactical advantage in combat

**Limitations:**
- Cannot be easily recharged or changed like true focus items
- Often requires re-enchantment after use
- Not suitable for mages relying heavily on magic
- Best used for single-purpose emergency spells
